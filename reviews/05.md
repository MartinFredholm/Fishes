# Review 05: Cephalofoil, pectoral and pelvic fins

## Round 1

**Verdict:** fail

### Blocking findings
1. [R15 / criterion 1] On the production 24-node spine, the pelvic attachment is off the arc by more than 1 mm in normal turns. `placePart` linearly interpolates between nodes, so a point between nodes lies on the chord, not on the arc. s = 0.54 falls at t = 0.42 between nodes 12 and 13, near the chord's midpoint. Measured: 1.38 mm at radius 1.5 m, 3.31 mm at 0.625 m (v_min / r_max). Cruise turns are 1.25 m (v_cruise / r_max), so this happens in ordinary swimming. The pectoral (t = 0.98) and the cephalofoil (on node 0) pass. The implementer's tests use 51 nodes so that every attachment s is a node, which hides the problem. Interpretation: this assumes "its s position" means the point on the curved spine shape (the testing approach checks spines "in a known shape (arc)"), not a point on the polyline between nodes. If you mean the polyline, this finding falls away. Reproduce: `./build/shark_body_tests -tc="review 05*"`.
2. [Comments] Two unnecessary comments, listed below.

### Unnecessary comments
- `shark_body.cpp:143`: "The root runs along the body edge from root_s to root_end_s" restates the vertices returned below it (`{0, root_half}` … `{root_end_s - root_s, halfWidth(root_end_s)}`). Keep only the why: the spine vertex closes the fin over the body so no gap opens in bends.
- `shark_body.h:33`: "Indices into BodyShape::rigid_parts." The code can say this. Nest the enum in `BodyShape`, or give the parts named fields.

### Non-blocking notes
- `placePart` interpolates the normals of nodes j and j + 1 linearly and then normalizes them. The orientation error is small, but it is a second chord approximation next to finding 1. One fix for both: interpolate along the local circle through three nodes, or use a cubic Hermite through the nodes.
- `FinShape::span` is measured from the body edge at root_s (as the comment states). The spec does not say where span is measured from. It is a reasonable reading, but it is an assumption for the user's photo check in issue 06.
- The cephalofoil's wing-tip vertex with the largest lateral offset is at back 0.07 L, and the rearmost vertex at 0.08 L sits at 0.13 L lateral. That fits "swept back to s ≈ 0.08". The comment flags the leading-edge curve as a guess, which is good.
- `rigid_parts_tests.cpp`: the comment describing `side` is above `FinSpec`, but `side` is a parameter of `checkFin`. It belongs above `checkFin`.
- The `CHECK(shape.rigid_parts.empty())` in `body_shape_tests.cpp` and `review_04_tests.cpp` was replaced by a check that no rigid-part vertex lies behind s = 0.75. This change is needed now that parts exist, and it still tests R14 ("no separate caudal shape"). It is not a weakened test.
- Criterion 5 (visual run: reads as a hammerhead from above) is not verified by this review. It is the user's check.

### Tests added
- `tests/review_05_tests.cpp` (registered in `CMakeLists.txt`): on a 24-node arc spine (radius 6, 3, 1.5 and 0.625 m, both bend directions), each rigid part's attachment vertex lies within 1 mm of the arc point at its s. It fails at s = 0.54 for 1.5 m and 0.625 m.

### Verified
- Build is clean. 34 existing test cases pass (`ctest` and `shark_body_tests`).
- R15: five parts, no dorsal fins. The implementer's tests check attachment, orientation and shape values on 51-node straight and arc spines.
- R17: the strip and every fan are drawn with `SHARK_COLOR` {140, 128, 106}, with no shading. The fan winding test covers back-face culling.

## Round 2

**Verdict:** pass

### Blocking findings
None.

### Unnecessary comments
None. Every comment in the diff gives a frame or units (`PartVertex`, test `Local`), a source (shape values table), a sign convention (`side`, `bend`) or a non-obvious why (Hermite sag, the spine vertex that closes the fin, mirrored winding, node 17 = s 0.74).

### Non-blocking notes
- Cephalofoil: the outermost points (±0.135 L) sit at s = 0.07, and the s = 0.08 vertex is at ±0.13 L. The spec says "wing tips swept back to s ≈ 0.08", so this fits, but the true tip is 0.01 L further forward than the table suggests. Check it in the issue 06 photo comparison.
- Each part is rigid and placed by the tangent at its leading s only. In the tightest turn (R = 0.625 m), the pectoral root end lies about 0.18²/(2·0.625) ≈ 26 mm off the body edge at s = 0.32. The spine vertex hides any gap, so this is a reading of R15 ("attached at their s positions", one per part), not a defect.
- `halfWidth(s)` duplicates the inline `interpolateSmooth(WIDTH_PROFILE, s) * length / 2` in `buildBodyShape` (one is in fractions of L, the other in meters). `buildBodyShape` could use `halfWidth(s) * length`.
- Unrelated to issue 05: at v_min with r = ±0.8 rad/s, the body update (issues 03/04) sometimes folds the spine at nodes 13–14, with turn angles of 1.2–1.6 rad between adjacent segments (for example r = 0.8, frame 481; r = −0.8, frame 592 in `tests/review_05_round2_tests.cpp`'s loop). Segment lengths stay correct, so the existing R10 tests do not catch it. It will likely show as a kink on screen in slow tight turns.

### Tests added
- `tests/review_05_round2_tests.cpp` (registered in `CMakeLists.txt`): on a swum, waved spine at v_min with r = +0.8, −0.8 and 0, the attachments lie at s = 0, 0.26 and 0.54 within 1 mm (2 mm for the pelvic distance to node 12). The cephalofoil midline vertex lies along the nose tangent within 2°, using a three-node circle as the tangent estimate. Left and right pectoral fins lie on their own sides. Frames where the spine is folded (see the last note) are skipped.
