# Handoff 05: Cephalofoil, pectoral and pelvic fins

## Built
- `buildBodyShape` now fills `rigid_parts` with the cephalofoil, the left and right pectoral fins and the left and right pelvic fins. There are no dorsal fins.
- Each part is rigid. Its outline is a constant in a local frame (distance back along the tangent, offset to screen-left, fractions of L). It is placed at one attachment s and oriented by the spine's left normal there. Between nodes, position and normal are interpolated linearly over node index, using the same uniform s_i = i/(n − 1) as the outline.
  - Cephalofoil: attached at s = 0. One 10-vertex polygon: leading edge at s = 0, span 0.27, rearmost tip points at s = 0.08, midline trailing edge at s = 0.06. The leading-edge points between the midline and the tips are guesses.
  - Fins: attached on the spine at root_s (pectoral 0.26, pelvic 0.54). Vertices: the attachment point, the body edge at root_s, the tip at tip_s and half-width at root_s plus span, then the body edge at root_end_s. The vertex on the spine closes the fin over the body, so no gap opens in bends. Root half-widths come from `WIDTH_PROFILE`, so retuning the width in 06 keeps the fin roots on the body edge.
  - Right parts are mirrored with all but the first vertex reversed, which keeps the winding and the fan centre.
- `drawShark` in `fishes.cpp` draws each part with `DrawTriangleFan` in `SHARK_COLOR`, after the outline strip.
- `tests/rigid_parts_tests.cpp`: R15 attachment, orientation and shape for each part (straight spines, and arcs with R = 3, 1.5 and 0.625 m bending both ways, each with 51 nodes and the production 24 nodes), the part count with no dorsal fins, and the winding check that keeps parts from being culled.

## Interfaces provided
```cpp
struct BodyShape {
    enum RigidPart { CEPHALOFOIL, PECTORAL_LEFT, PECTORAL_RIGHT, PELVIC_LEFT, PELVIC_RIGHT, RIGID_PART_COUNT };
    std::vector<Vector2> left, right;               // unchanged
    std::vector<std::vector<Vector2>> rigid_parts;  // indexed by RigidPart
};
BodyShape buildBodyShape(const std::vector<Vector2> &spine, float length); // unchanged
```
- Each polygon's first vertex is its attachment point on the spine. It is a triangle fan around that vertex, wound like the outline strip's (right, left, next right) triangles.
- The type of `rigid_parts` is unchanged. The enum and the contract are additions. Refer to parts as `BodyShape::PECTORAL_LEFT` and so on.

## Deviations
- Two earlier assertions, `rigid_parts.empty()` in `tests/body_shape_tests.cpp` (R14) and in `tests/review_04_tests.cpp`, were replaced, with the user's approval. They now check that there are `RIGID_PART_COUNT` parts and that no part vertex lies behind s = 0.75. Reason: they encoded "empty until 05". R14's actual requirement is that there is no separate caudal shape.
- "Span" is measured from the body edge at the fin's root_s. The spec does not say where span is measured from.

## Not verified
- The Hermite is checked against true arcs and straight lines only. On a waved spine, the curve between nodes is a smooth interpolation, not a known shape. No test checks it there.
- Parts are rigid over their length. In the tightest turn, the fin's root-trailing vertex can sit about 26 mm (about 1.3 px) off the bent body edge on the outer side of the turn. This is an estimate and was not measured.
- The "no dorsal fins" and winding tests passed as soon as they were written. The winding test was mutation-checked: making `mirrored` keep its order failed it, with 32 assertions.
- Visual: one screenshot after 6 s. Two sharks show the cephalofoil and both fin pairs in one grey-bronze fill. Whether the proportions match a real hammerhead is for the user to check (issue 06).

## Fix passes
- Round 1: rigid parts between nodes now follow a cubic Hermite through the nodes, not the chord. On the 24-node arc at R = 0.625 m, the pelvic attachment error dropped from 3.31 mm to 0.012 mm (largest error over all of `review 05`). The fin and cephalofoil tests now also run on 24-node spines. `RigidPart` is nested in `BodyShape`, and the fin-root comment keeps only the why. The `side` comment in the tests moved to `checkFin`.
